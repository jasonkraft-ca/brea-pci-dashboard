Map View Options

Three ways the 533-street pavement dataset could be mapped, each rendered from the real data so you can compare them directly. 478 streets have usable geometry.

Option A — Inline SVG

Every street drawn as a vector path, coloured by its PCI band. The city outline and the 16 major corridors are drawn in for orientation, so it reads as Brea without a tiled basemap. No libraries, no network, no API key. Drag to pan, scroll or use +/− to zoom, hover a street for detail.

Trade-off: self-contained and fast, matches the dashboard exactly, and can share the existing filters. No aerial or business context — you orient by the corridor labels. Adds ~250 KB to the page.

Option B — Leaflet + OpenStreetMap basemap

The same street lines drawn over a real slippy map. You get every landmark, park, parcel and business name for free, plus familiar pan/zoom. Click a street for its detail popup.

Trade-off: by far the best context, but it pulls Leaflet from a CDN and tiles from OSM's servers on every view — the page stops being self-contained and needs a network connection. Public OSM tiles also aren't licensed for heavy production traffic; a real deployment would need a tile provider.

Option D — Condition heatmap

Instead of individual streets, pavement is binned into 250 m cells and each cell is coloured by its area-weighted average PCI. This answers "where is the bad pavement clustered?" at a glance — the per-street detail stays in the table.

Trade-off: the clearest overview of where the problems are, and the smallest payload of the three. You lose the ability to find one specific street on the map, so it works best alongside the list rather than replacing it.

Recommendation: A as the main map — it keeps the dashboard a single self-contained file that works anywhere, and the corridor labels carry enough orientation. D is cheap enough (~30 KB) to add as a small overview alongside it. Choose B only if seeing pavement against real parcels and businesses matters more than staying dependency-free.

Caveat for all three: 55 of 533 streets have no usable OpenStreetMap geometry and cannot be drawn. Whichever option we pick should state that count on the map so it never looks like those streets simply have no problems.
